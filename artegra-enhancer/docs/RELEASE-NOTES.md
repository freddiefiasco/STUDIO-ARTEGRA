# Artegra Enhancer (Beta) 5.0.2 Beta 1

**Two engines.** This release has two engines. The Classic engine is the one in 5.0.1: it makes every upscale it can make on your computer, with the same passes and settings as 5.0.1, apart from the Protect change below. The new Large-image engine makes only the sizes the Classic engine cannot make on your computer. It runs Redux only; Bolt stays on the Classic engine, with the limits it has in 5.0.1. The plan for each image and the caption of its result say which engine runs it.

**What runs now.** On a 64 GB PC with one RTX 5090, the Large-image engine makes results of up to about 213 MP that 5.0.1 refused there: Redux 4× of 3500 × 2188 and 2800 × 3500 renders, and 6× and 8× of smaller images, such as 8× of a 1280 × 1600 image. On our test PC, 4× of a 3500 × 2188 render took about 84 minutes and an 8× result of about 213 MP about 2 hours 20 minutes. The size limits follow one rule: a computer with more memory makes at least the largest size made on a computer with less memory. On a 128 GB PC the Classic engine already makes every size up to about 150 MP, so the Large-image engine adds results longer than 16,384 px on a side and results above 150 MP, up to about 213 MP. Workstation runs reached 131 MP; above that, the 128 GB figure comes from the 64 GB PC's runs, and the 128 GB PC uses the 64 GB PC's settings for those sizes. So a 128 GB PC now makes 4× of a 2800 × 3500 render (157 MP), as a 64 GB PC does. 6× and 8× of a 3500 × 2188 or 2800 × 3500 render are refused on every computer.

**How the Large-image engine differs.** It works on the image in bands and keeps the image in work files on the work drive. It still needs more memory for larger outputs, and a size that does not fit this computer's memory is refused before it starts. Before an image starts, its plan gives the estimated time on your graphics card, the output size, the free disk space its work files need and the memory class of your computer. A Large-image upscale can restart the image engine between passes and before the last step of a large pass, so that the memory the last step held is given back; the estimated time counts each restart.

**The same result where both engines run.** The Large-image engine runs the same enhancement steps as the Classic engine over the whole image. On our test PCs, at the sizes both engines can run, its results were pixel-identical to the Classic engine's. It also keeps the Classic engine's look, including its edge halos. That comparison covered nine cases at 2× and 4×, each checked at 1:1 by two reviewers who did not know which engine made which image.

**The first Large-image upscale on a computer.** Unless your kind of processor was measured before this release, the app must first measure how your computer's processor runs the Large-image engine's finish. Until then the larger Large-image sizes are refused, and the message ends by saying that the app will measure the processor when it is free; queue the image again once it has, and Activity says when. A smaller Large-image upscale is queued at once and waits for the measurement instead. The measurement uses the processor, not the graphics card, and no other image starts while it runs. On our test PC's processor it took about 22 to 25 minutes for 4× and 8× of a 3500 × 2188 render; a larger image or a slower processor takes longer. It can run again for a size it has not measured yet, or after an update changes the image engine. If it cannot finish, the largest Large-image sizes are refused on this computer, and the refusal says that the processor has not been measured.

**What both engines do, as in 5.0.1.** The finish puts small areas the enhancement invented back to the x4 upscaling model's picture, and carries the source's broad tones (its low frequencies) into the result. Both engines leave the light and dark halos along strong edges of geometry that 5.0.1 leaves; reducing them is planned for a later version. Halos, and the small changes the enhancement makes to content such as planting, grow with each pass, so a 6× or 8× result shows more of both than a 2× or 4× one. Check fine planting and dark edges against the sky at 100 % before you use a large result.

**Large Protect passes.** With Protect on, a pass whose input is larger than about 33 MP now finds the protected areas with a step that enlarges only the masks it keeps. At a 37 MP pass on an RTX 5090 (32 GB), that step used 12.8 GB of graphics memory where 5.0.1's step used 26.4 GB, and the masks were the same. In 5.0.1 a 66 MP pass of this kind spilled about 9 GB into shared graphics memory and took about twice its expected time; a pass that large has not been run again with 5.0.2. On a card of 24 GB or less a pass can still use shared memory, as the next section says.

**Graphics cards with 24 GB or less.** Graphics cards with 24 GB or less accept every upscale 5.0.1 accepts on them. With Protect on, the protected areas are found by the step that enlarges only the masks it keeps whenever the usual step would not fit the card; the masks are the same. An image that still needs more graphics memory than the card has is accepted as in 5.0.1. While it waits and while it is queued, the line under the picture begins with "May run slowly" and the graphics memory it needs; the rest of the note, that it can take longer than the time shown, is on the same line and shows in full when you point at the line if the window cuts it short. The Activity log says so when it starts; as in 5.0.1, a pass that takes far longer than expected is stopped. On a PC with a small and a large graphics card, both cards take images, as in 5.0.1: an image waits for a larger card it fits only while that card is free, and otherwise the free card runs it, with the note from its start. How much longer such an image takes has not been measured, and these upscales have not yet been run on a card of 24 GB or less.

**Finish times.** Finish times now count the work on large inputs and outputs. A Redux 6× of a 2350 × 1762 image with Protect on said 81 min in 5.0.1 and took 115 min on an RTX 5090; 5.0.2 says 119 min for it. In a second check, the last pass of a Redux 6× that 5.0.1 said would take 15 min took 26 min; 5.0.2 says 27 min.

**32 GB and 48 GB PCs.** Setup now accepts PCs with 32 GB of memory or more (31 GB as Windows reports it). A 32 GB PC makes small upscales on the Classic engine, such as Redux 2× of a 1920 × 1080 image and 4× of a 960 × 540 image; larger upscales need more memory, and the Large-image engine needs a 48 GB PC. A 48 GB PC also makes Redux 4× of 3500 × 2188 and 2800 × 3500 renders on the Large-image engine. No 32 GB or 48 GB PC has run 5.0.2. A 32 GB PC's sizes are the app's memory estimate at its default memory limit (29.2 GB). A 48 GB PC's Large-image limit, about 157 MP, comes from runs on a 64 GB PC held to a 48 GB PC's memory limit (45.2 GB).

**Memory figures.** The Large-image engine keeps 1 GB of the app's memory spare, so on a 64 GB PC its messages name 58 GB where the Classic engine's name 59 GB.

**Updating.** Install over 5.0.1 Beta 1: your images, output folder, settings, waiting images and enhancement files are kept, and setup checks the models without downloading them again. 5.0.2 adds no new control or setting to the app.

Targets native x64 Windows 10 22H2 or Windows 11 22H2+, with an NVIDIA RTX card and 32 GB of system memory or more (64 GB for the larger upscales). Physical verification remains Windows 11 and RTX 5090. # Artegra Enhancer (Beta) 5.0.1 Beta 1

**What runs now.** On a 64 GB PC with one RTX 5090, Redux and Bolt make 2× of 3500 × 2188 renders and Redux makes 4× of a 1280 × 1600 image, all at full quality. Redux also makes 2× of a 2800 × 3500 render, at the lighter setting its size needs. 5.0.0 Beta 1 refused them for memory. Every image 5.0.0 Beta 1 could make is planned with the same passes and made exactly as before.

**Why it refused them.** It estimated 2× of a 3500 × 2188 render at 61.5 GB, against measured peaks of 51 GB in Bolt and 46 GB in Redux, and it kept back 10 % of the computer's memory, which left 55 GB on a 64 GB PC. The estimate now comes from peaks measured on RTX 5090 PCs, plus a 3 % margin, and the app keeps back 4 %, and at least 2.5 GB, which leaves 59 GB.

**When memory is short.** The app first tries a lighter setting at the size you chose, and the image's caption says so. Only when even that does not fit is the image refused, with the amount it needs. Bolt says when memory, not size, is the reason.

**What is still refused.** Redux 4× of a 3500 × 2188 render and Redux 6× or 8× of a 1280 × 1600 image need about 65 to 90 GB, so a 64 GB PC refuses them and a 128 GB PC runs them. 6× and 8× of a 3500 × 2188 render and 4× of a 2800 × 3500 render are over the engine's output limit of about 150 MP, so every computer refuses them; lifting that limit is planned. See the [known issues](../KNOWN-ISSUES.md).

**Updating.** Install over 5.0.0 Beta 1: your images, output folder, settings, waiting images and enhancement files are kept. If you changed `ram_cap_gb` in `settings.json` by hand as a workaround, 5.0.1 keeps your value; to use the new default, remove that entry as the [installation guide](../INSTALL.md#update-from-500-beta-1) describes.

## 5.0.0 Beta 1

This is the first beta of Artegra Enhancer. It brings the enhancement tab up to date with all its latest fixes, renames the modes, and adds an update check and a Windows uninstaller.

**Redux and Bolt.** Standard is now Redux, for finals; Fast is now Bolt, for quick drafts. Each has a small mark: Redux's stacked courses and Bolt's strike, beside the names and on every queued image and result. A Bolt result is saved as `name_cu2x_bolt.png`; older `_fast` results are still recognised.

**The enhancement tab.** The tab, the service behind it and the finish times have all their latest fixes. Bolt is offered only once its files are checked. On two identical RTX 5090 cards a single Bolt image uses both cards; everywhere else each Bolt image uses one card, and the finish time says so. Saved images are made by the same engine and recipes as in the alphas; only the live preview's fallback changed.

**Check for updates.** In Settings and About. It asks GitHub only when you click it and never downloads or installs anything.

**Uninstall.** From Installed apps, the Start menu or Settings. It closes only the app's own processes, never removes your images, and offers to also remove the downloaded enhancement files and the settings and logs, both off by default.

**Upgrading.** Install over alpha 4: your models, settings, outputs and memory choice are kept. Setup shows the terms page again and checks the models you have; it does not download them again.

Targets native x64 Windows 10 22H2 or Windows 11 22H2+, with an NVIDIA RTX card and 64 GB of system memory. Physical verification remains Windows 11 and RTX 5090.
